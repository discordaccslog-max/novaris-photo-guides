What's inside

Contents of the guide

The guide is organized into the sections below. Every section is included in both the US Letter and A4 files.

Shot lists by session type

Separate shot lists for headshot, family, and couples sessions, laid out as checklists you can mark off during the session and add to on the day.

Posing reference diagrams

Line-drawn diagrams of standing, seated, and group arrangements, used as a quick visual reference for positioning people in the frame.

Natural-light setups

Top-down diagrams of common natural-light situations, such as window light, open shade, and backlight, showing subject, camera, and light direction.

One- and two-light setups

Diagrams for single-light and two-light portraits that mark light placement, angle, modifier type, and the position of reflectors or backgrounds.

Client session planning sheets

Worksheets for recording session details ahead of time: people in the session, location, timing, wardrobe notes, and must-have shots.

Page previews

Representative pages

Illustrations of three representative page layouts from this guide. They show the layout and structure of each page type.

Fit

Who it's for, and who it isn't

A good fit for

  • Photographers who run headshot, family, or couples sessions and want a printed shot list and setup reference on hand
  • Anyone who plans sessions on paper and wants consistent worksheets for each client
  • Photographers who work with natural light, a single light, or a simple two-light kit

Not the right fit for

  • Anyone looking for a course or lessons: this guide is reference pages, not instruction
  • Photographers who need editable or fillable templates: the PDFs are flat and meant for printing
  • Large studio productions built around complex multi-light rigs
  • Anyone hoping for feedback on their photos: the guide contains no critique
